Here are the **Top Mental Math Patterns Founders Memorize** so they can calculate pricing, scaling, margins, and growth numbers instantly during conversations.

These aren’t random tricks — they’re the exact number patterns used repeatedly in SaaS, infrastructure, marketing, and finance discussions.

---

## 1) The Common Multiplication Table Number Patterns (Aka Number Anchors)

This is the most powerful shortcut.

If you see:

- price ends in **5 or 50**
    
- cost ends in **20, 200, 2000**
    

Your brain can instantly map (from a childhood of memorizing multiplication table numbers):

> 5 × 4 = 20 → answer contains 4

Examples:

- $50 pricing, $2000 cost → 40 users
    
- $5 CAC, $200 revenue → 40 customers
    

This works because you’re matching the **base pattern**, then scaling magnitude.

---

## 2) Magnitude Recognition

This is what strong operators actually use.

You don’t compute.

You **recognize scale patterns.**

Example:

$50 price vs $2000 cost

Your brain sees:

- 50 → contains 5
    
- 2000 → contains 20
    

You already know:

> 5 × 4 = 20

So answer must contain 4 scaled:

→ 40 users

This is the fastest method possible.

  

---

## 3) Rule of 10 Scaling

Multiplying or dividing by 10 is just moving the decimal.

Memorize:

- ×10 → add zero
    
- ÷10 → remove zero
    

Examples:

- $72 × 10 → 720
    
- 2,400 ÷ 10 → 240
    

Founders constantly scale numbers up/down this way to estimate scenarios quickly.

---

## 4) 1% = Move Decimal Two Places

Used constantly for growth, churn, fees, and conversion.

Examples:

- 1% of 2,000 = 20
    
- 5% of 2,000 = 100
    
- 10% of 2,000 = 200
    

Once you know 1%, you can compute any percentage fast.

---

## 5) 50% + 25% + 10% + 5% Pattern

Instead of calculating percentages directly, break them into known chunks.

Memorize:

- 50% = half
    
- 25% = quarter
    
- 10% = divide by 10
    
- 5% = half of 10%
    

Example:  
35% of 2000

= 10% + 10% + 10% + 5%  
= 200 + 200 + 200 + 100 = **700**

---

## 6) Monthly ↔ Daily Conversion Shortcut

Used for server costs, ad spend, SaaS pricing.

Rule:

> Monthly ÷ 30 ≈ daily

Examples:

- $900/mo → $30/day
    
- $300/mo → $10/day
    
- $720/mo → $24/day
    

Reverse:

> Daily × 30 ≈ monthly

---

## 7) The 2× / 3× / 5× Revenue Multipliers

Founders constantly check unit economics:

Memorize what multiples look like instantly.

Example:

- Cost = $20
    
- 3× markup → $60
    
- 5× markup → $100
    

No calculator — just pattern recognition.

---

## 8) The 80/20 Heuristic Estimation

Not exact math — strategic math.

If:

- 20% of users generate 80% load
    
- 20% of customers generate 80% revenue
    

Then you can estimate system or revenue impact quickly without full data.

---

## 9) Concurrency Reality Rule

Used for infrastructure sizing.

Memorize:

> Only ~10–20% of users active at once

So:

- 100 users ≠ 100 simultaneous jobs
    
- It’s closer to 10–20 concurrent
    

This prevents overbuying servers.

---

## 10) Break-Even Formula Shortcut

Instead of full equation:

> Users needed = cost ÷ price

Memorize common divisions:

|   |   |   |
|---|---|---|
|Cost|Price|Users|
|1000|50|20|
|2000|50|40|
|3000|50|60|

You start recognizing patterns instantly.

### 13) 1/3 and 2/3 Shortcut

You’ll use thirds constantly in business splits, margins, and revenue share.

Memorize:

- 1/3 ≈ 33%
    
- 2/3 ≈ 66%
    

So:

- 1/3 of 3000 ≈ 1000
    
- 2/3 of 3000 ≈ 2000

## B) Cost + Infrastructure Thinking

---

### 14) The Hour → Month Conversion Constant

Memorize this:

> Hourly cost × 720 ≈ Monthly cost

Because:

> 24 × 30 ≈ 720

Examples:

- $0.50/hr → ~$360/mo
    
- $1/hr → ~$720/mo
    
- $2/hr → ~$1,440/mo
    

This is one of the most powerful infra mental shortcuts.

---

### 15) The 70% Utilization Rule

Most systems shouldn’t run above ~70% sustained load.

So if a server handles:

> 10 jobs/sec max

Safe capacity = **7 jobs/sec**

This prevents founders from overestimating capacity.

---

### 16) The “Always Add 30%” Rule

Real costs are almost always underestimated.

So mentally adjust:

> estimated cost × 1.3

Example:

- Estimate = $2000
    
- Realistic expectation ≈ $2600
    

This is a real operator heuristic.

## D) Growth + Scaling Instinct Patterns

---

### 19) Linear vs Exponential Recognition

Founders mentally test:

Is growth linear or compounding?

Linear example:  
+100 users per month

Exponential example:  
×1.2 growth per month

After 6 months:

- linear → +600
    
- exponential → ×3
    

Strong founders instantly sense which trajectory they’re on.

---

### 20) The “Half / Double Sanity Check”

Before trusting a number, quickly ask:

> If this were half or double, would it still make sense?

This catches:

- bad estimates
    
- unrealistic projections
    
- incorrect math
