Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) is a fundamental financial metric that represents the direct costs associated with producing goods or services that a company sells. Let me explain how COGS is calculated and provide some practical examples.

## Basic COGS Formula

The standard formula for calculating COGS is:

COGS = Beginning Inventory + Purchases - Ending Inventory

This shows that COGS accounts for the cost of inventory that was actually sold during a period.

## Components of COGS

COGS typically includes:

- Raw materials
- Direct labor costs
- Manufacturing overhead directly tied to production
- Shipping and freight-in costs
- Storage costs for materials and finished goods

COGS excludes indirect expenses like:

- Marketing and advertising
- Sales team salaries
- Distribution costs
- Administrative expenses

## Business Examples

### 1. Retail Business Example

Let's say a clothing store has the following inventory data for Q1:

- Beginning inventory: $50,000
- Purchases during Q1: $75,000
- Ending inventory: $40,000

COGS = $50,000 + $75,000 - $40,000 = $85,000

This means the store sold $85,000 worth of inventory at cost during Q1.

### 2. Manufacturing Company Example

A furniture manufacturer has:

- Beginning inventory: $100,000
- Raw materials purchased: $200,000
- Direct labor: $150,000
- Manufacturing overhead: $75,000
- Ending inventory: $120,000

COGS = $100,000 + ($200,000 + $150,000 + $75,000) - $120,000 = $405,000

### 3. Restaurant Example

For a restaurant:

- Beginning food inventory: $5,000
- Food purchases: $25,000
- Kitchen staff wages: $20,000
- Kitchen utilities: $3,000
- Ending food inventory: $4,000

COGS = $5,000 + ($25,000 + $20,000 + $3,000) - $4,000 = $49,000

## COGS Calculation Methods

### FIFO (First-In, First-Out)

Assumes that the oldest inventory items are sold first. During inflation, this typically results in lower COGS and higher profit.

### LIFO (Last-In, First-Out)

Assumes that the newest inventory items are sold first. During inflation, this typically results in higher COGS and lower taxable income.

### Weighted Average Cost

Takes the weighted average of all units available for sale during the period.
