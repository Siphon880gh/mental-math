### 📊 Understanding LTV (Lifetime Value) Metrics

**LTV**, or **Customer Lifetime Value**, is a key business metric that estimates the **total revenue a business expects to earn from a customer throughout their relationship**.

---

### 🔢 Basic Formula

LTV is often calculated as:

> **LTV = Average Purchase Value × Purchase Frequency × Customer Lifespan**

Or for subscription businesses:

> **LTV = Average Monthly Revenue per Customer × Average Customer Lifespan (in months)**

---

### 🧲 How LTV Gets Easier to Calculate With Marketing Commitment

When customers commit through **marketing-driven offers** (e.g. 6-month prepay, annual plan, onboarding funnel), **you get clearer data** and reduce variability in churn. Let's compare:

|**Scenario**|**Month-to-Month (No Commitment)**|**Marketing-Driven Commitment**|
|---|---|---|
|**Churn rate**|Highly variable|More predictable|
|**Customer lifespan**|Uncertain|Known (e.g. 6 or 12 months)|
|**Revenue per customer**|Volatile|Locked in|
|**LTV accuracy**|Low|High|
|**Cohort predictability**|Weak|Strong|

---

### 🧠 Example

#### Month-to-Month Plan

- $30/month
    
- Churn: 20% monthly (average customer stays ~5 months)
    
- → LTV = $30 × 5 = **$150**
    

But churn may spike in bad months (e.g., after summer), making the LTV **unpredictable**.

#### Marketing-Fueled Offer

- Annual plan: $300 upfront with a bonus
    
- Customers acquired with this promo stay longer, complain less, and don’t churn early
    
- → LTV = **$300** (immediately realized, predictable)
    

---

### 💡 Takeaway

> **Commitment marketing (e.g. prepay, bundles, bonuses) simplifies LTV math and makes your business more predictable.**

This clearer LTV helps you:

- Set **realistic CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)** targets
- Predict **cash flow**
- Justify **marketing spend** confidently
- Raise money or scale with confidence
