# Dilution and post-money

A priced round:

> post-money = pre-money + cash in  
> percent sold ≈ cash / post-money

## Example

Pre-money $8M, raise $2M → post-money **$10M**. You sold 2/10 = **20%**.

Founders who owned 100% now own 80% (if no option-pool shuffle).

## Option pool from pre-money

If the pool is created **before** the round, founders dilute twice (pool, then investor). Mental check: a 10% pool carved from pre-money on the example is 10% of $8M = $800k of extra paper, which is not the same as 10% of post.

You do not need the full cap table in your head. You need: **cash ÷ post-money ≈ investor percent.**

## SAFE / uncapped

If there is no price yet, you cannot finish this trick. Ask for a cap or a price.

## Conversation use

“We raised 3 on 12 pre” → post 15, sold **20%**. “We raised 3 at 12 post” is sold 3/12 = **25%**. Listen for pre vs post.

## Worked example

Pre-money $14M, raising $3.5M, and the investor wants a 10% option pool counted in the post-money.

1. Post-money: 14 + 3.5 = **$17.5M**.
2. Investor: 3.5 ÷ 17.5 = **20%**.
3. Founders and earlier holders: 100 − 20 − 10 = **70%**. Without the pool they would hold 80%. The pool came out of their side, not the investor's.

## Why it works

New shares are created for the cash, so every existing slice shrinks in proportion. The investor's percent is just their dollars over the value after the money arrives. Everything else is bookkeeping about who absorbs the pool.

## Where it goes wrong

- Dividing by pre: 3.5 ÷ 14 = 25% overstates what was sold.
- Treating a SAFE cap as a price: the cap is the most you would convert at, not the round's price.
- Subtracting points across rounds: a founder at 50% who sells 20% in the next round holds 50 × 0.8 = 40%, not 30%. Multiply stakes.

## Same idea, new numbers

- Pre 20, raise 5 → post 25, sold 20%
- Raise 1, post 5 → sold 20%
