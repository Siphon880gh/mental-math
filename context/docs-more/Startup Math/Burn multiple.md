# Burn multiple

How many dollars of **net burn** did you spend to add a dollar of **ARR**?

> burn multiple ≈ net burn / net new ARR

Net burn is cash out minus cash in for the period. Net new ARR is ending ARR minus starting ARR (for that period).

## Shapes

- 1×: spend $1 to add $1 ARR (efficient)
- 2×: common while scaling
- 5×+: you are buying revenue expensively — or the ARR add is tiny

Example: burn $2M this year, ARR went from $3M to $4M (+$1M) → multiple = **2×**.

## Sign

If ARR fell, the multiple is meaningless or infinite. Fix retention first.

## Conversation use

“We only burned 800k and added 200k ARR” → **4×**. Not a badge.

## Worked example

In one quarter, gross spend was $2.6M and cash collected was $0.4M. ARR went from $5.1M to $5.9M.

1. Net burn: 2.6 − 0.4 = **$2.2M**.
2. Net new ARR: 5.9 − 5.1 = **$0.8M**.
3. Multiple: 2.2 ÷ 0.8 = **2.75×**. Scaling territory, drifting away from efficient.

## Why it works

Burn multiple asks how much cash it takes to build one dollar of recurring revenue. ARR is what the company is valued on, so the ratio is a price tag on growth. Both figures are net: churn eats into net new ARR, so a leaky product shows up as a worse multiple even when gross sales look fine.

## Where it goes wrong

- Gross spend instead of net burn: 2.6 ÷ 0.8 = 3.25× penalizes you for revenue you collected.
- New bookings instead of net new ARR: ignoring churn hides the leak and flatters the multiple.
- Mixing periods: a quarter's burn over a full year's ARR add understates the multiple by about 4×.

## Same idea, new numbers

- Burn 1.2M, new ARR 600k → 2×
- Burn 500k, new ARR 500k → 1×
