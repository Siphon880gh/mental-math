# Net revenue retention

**NRR** (net revenue retention) asks: this cohort’s revenue a year later, including expansion, minus contraction and churn, as a percent of starting revenue.

> NRR ≈ ending revenue from the same customers ÷ starting revenue of those customers

GRR is the same idea **without** expansion (upsell). GRR cannot exceed 100%. NRR can.

## Mental shapes

- NRR 100%: expansion exactly replaced churn
- NRR 120%: the base grew 20% with no new logos
- NRR 80%: the existing book shrank 20%

If start MRR from a cohort is $100k and a year later those accounts are $90k plus $40k expansion = $130k → NRR = **130%**.

## Why it matters

High NRR means you can grow while buying fewer new customers. It is the “do we have a product that expands?” test.

## Conversation use

“We grow 10% month but NRR is 70%” — new logos are hiding a melting base. Do not celebrate the top-line alone.

## Worked example

A cohort starts the year at $240k ARR. Over the year those same accounts add $60k of expansion, contract by $18k, and churn $30k.

1. Ending revenue from the same accounts: 240 + 60 − 18 − 30 = **$252k**.
2. NRR: 252 ÷ 240 = 1.05 → **105%**.
3. GRR (drop the expansion): (240 − 18 − 30) ÷ 240 = 192 ÷ 240 = **80%**.

## Why it works

NRR holds the customer list fixed, so it isolates what the product does to accounts you already have. Above 100%, last year's customers alone grow revenue and every new logo lands on top. Below 100%, sales must refill a leaking bucket before the company grows at all.

## Where it goes wrong

- Adding new logos: if $84k of brand-new customers also signed, it stays out. Including it would read (252 + 84) ÷ 240 = 140%.
- Reporting the change instead of the index: "+5%" is the delta; NRR is 105%.
- Netting churn twice: contraction and churn are already inside the ending number. Do not subtract them again.

## Same idea, new numbers

- Start 50, end (same logos) 60 → 120%
- Start 50, end 40, no expansion → GRR 80%, NRR 80%
