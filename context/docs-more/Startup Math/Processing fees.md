# Processing fees

Card networks are not free. A common SAMPLE stand-in (not a live quote):

> fee ≈ 2.9% + $0.30 per successful charge

## Percent first, then the nickel

$10 charge: 2.9% ≈ $0.29, plus $0.30 → **≈ $0.59** (about 6%).  
$100 charge: 2.9% = $2.90 + $0.30 → **$3.20** (about 3.2%).

Small tickets **feel** a much higher percent because of the 30¢.

## Mental 3%

For smell tests, **3% of GMV** is a decent card-fee bucket unless you are all micro-payments (then budget more).

$50k processed ≈ **$1.5k** fees at 3%.

## Conversation use

A $5 digital good: 2.9% is 15¢ + 30¢ = 45¢ → **9%** gone before your COGS. Price or batch accordingly.

## Worked example

A shop runs 3,500 orders a month at a $32 average, at 2.9% + $0.30.

1. Volume: 3,500 × 32 = $112k. Percent part: 3% is 3,360, minus 0.1% (112) → **$3,248**.
2. Fixed part: 3,500 × 0.30 = **$1,050**.
3. Total ≈ **$4,298**, about 3.8% of volume. Higher than the 3% bucket because of the 30¢.

## Why it works

The percent scales with dollars; the 30¢ scales with the count of charges. Split the two, then add. Small tickets carry more of the fixed part, which is why a coffee shop's effective rate beats a furniture store's.

## Where it goes wrong

- Forgetting the fixed part: $3,248 alone understates fees by about a quarter.
- Applying 30¢ per dollar or per month instead of per charge.
- Assuming refunds return the fee: many processors keep it, so a refund costs you the fee as well.

## Same idea, new numbers

- $20 → 0.58 + 0.30 = $0.88
- $1,000 → $29 + $0.30 = $29.30
