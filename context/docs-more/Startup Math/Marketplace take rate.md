# Marketplace take rate

A marketplace earns a **cut of GMV** (gross merchandise value), not the full transaction.

> revenue ≈ GMV × take rate

Take rate 15% on $2M GMV → **$300k**.

## Two-sided check

Your take has to leave enough for the supply side. 30% take on a 10% margin category may empty the seller. Smell: take vs typical supplier margin.

## Mix

If 20% of GMV is a 10% take product and 80% is 20% take:

Blended take ≈ 0.2×10% + 0.8×20% = 2% + 16% = **18%**.

## Conversation use

“We’ll do $10M in volume” is not $10M revenue. Ask the take. At 12% that is **$1.2M**.

## Worked example

A booking marketplace handles 12,000 bookings a month at $85 average and keeps 12%.

1. GMV: 12,000 × 85 = **$1.02M**.
2. Revenue: 10% is 102k, 2% is 20.4k → **$122.4k**.
3. After about 3% processing on the full GMV: 122.4k − 30.6k ≈ **$92k**.

## Why it works

A marketplace sells the match, not the goods, so its revenue is a slice of volume. Compare marketplaces on revenue and on take: GMV can be pumped with discounts, while take shows pricing power and how much value the platform really adds.

## Where it goes wrong

- Quoting GMV as revenue: "a million a month" is about $122k of revenue here.
- Fees on revenue instead of GMV: processors charge on the whole payment, so 3% of GMV is a quarter of a 12% take.
- Blending by count instead of volume: weight each product's take by its share of GMV, not by its number of orders.

## Same idea, new numbers

- GMV 500k, take 8% → 40k
- GMV 1M, take 25% → 250k
