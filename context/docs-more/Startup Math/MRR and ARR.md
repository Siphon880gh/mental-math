# MRR and ARR

**MRR** is the monthly recurring run-rate. **ARR** is that year as if it lasted twelve months:

> ARR ≈ MRR × 12  
> MRR ≈ ARR ÷ 12

Twelve months. SaaS just names the yearly figure ARR.

## Examples

- $40/seat × 50 customers paying monthly → MRR = 2,000 → ARR = **$24k**
- $1.2M ARR → MRR = **$100k**

## What to exclude (mental hygiene)

Do not shove one-time setup fees, professional services, or a single hardware sale into MRR. If it does not recur, it is not MRR.

Annual prepay of $480 for a $40/mo plan is still $40 MRR (the cash arrived early; the run-rate did not 12×).

## Conversation use

“We’re at 80k MRR” → ARR is about **$1M**. “We did $1M last year” is **not** ARR if $400k was services.

## Worked example

A tool sells 320 seats at $45/mo and also billed a $6,000 onboarding fee this month.

1. Recurring part only: 320 × 45 = 14,400 → MRR **$14.4k**. The fee stays out.
2. ARR: 14.4k × 12. Do ×10 then add ×2: 144k + 28.8k = **$172.8k**, call it about $173k.
3. Round-trip check: 172.8k ÷ 12 = 14.4k. If the round trip does not land, one step slipped.

## Why it works

ARR is not a second measurement. It is the same monthly run-rate stated per year, so it can sit next to yearly costs, salaries, and valuations. Multiply by 12 only what will repeat next month without a new sale.

## Where it goes wrong

- Counting the onboarding fee: (14.4k + 6k) × 12 = $244.8k overstates ARR by $72k. One-time money ×12 is the classic inflated deck.
- Counting an annual prepay twelve times: a customer prepaying $540 for a $45/mo plan adds $45 of MRR, not $540.
- ×10 instead of ×12 when rushing: $14.4k → $144k undercounts by a sixth. Do ×10, then add the ×2.

## Same idea, new numbers

- $25 × 200 customers = $5k MRR = $60k ARR
- $360k ARR = $30k MRR
