# CAC payback

How many months until gross profit from a customer returns what you paid to acquire them?

> payback months ≈ CAC ÷ monthly gross profit per customer

Monthly gross profit ≈ price × gross margin. If margin is ~80% on a $50/mo plan, profit ≈ $40/mo. CAC $240 → 240/40 = **6 months**.

Healthy SaaS often aims for **≤12 months**. The original patterns file already names that bar; this lesson is the arithmetic.

## Quick path

1. CAC (ads + sales fully loaded for a period ÷ new customers)
2. Monthly revenue per customer
3. Haircut for COGS (hosting, payments, support)
4. Divide

If you ignore COGS you understate payback (looks better than it is).

## Conversation use

CAC $120, price $20/mo, ignore COGS → 6 months. If COGS is $5, profit $15 → **8 months**. Still under 12.

## Worked example

Paid ads plus the sales team cost $52k last month and brought 130 customers on a $70/mo plan with a 70% gross margin.

1. CAC: 52,000 ÷ 130 = **$400**.
2. Monthly gross profit: 70 × 0.7 = **$49**.
3. Payback: 400 ÷ 49 ≈ **8.2 months**. Inside the 12-month bar.

## Why it works

CAC is cash spent up front. Gross profit is what each month returns after the cost of serving the customer. Dividing gives the months the money is tied up, which is a working-capital question: the shorter the payback, the sooner growth funds itself.

## Where it goes wrong

- Price instead of gross profit: 400 ÷ 70 ≈ 5.7 months looks rosier than the truth.
- Leaving salaries out of CAC: ads alone undercount. Include the sales team's loaded cost for the same period.
- Ignoring churn: if customers stay about 7 months, an 8-month payback never pays back at all.

## Same idea, new numbers

- CAC 300, profit 25/mo → 12 months
- CAC 90, profit 30/mo → 3 months
