# Fermi TAM

A **Fermi estimate** is a stack of round numbers that should land on the right **order of magnitude**. It is the TAM/SAM conversation without a consultant deck.

## Method

1. How many possible customers (people, companies, or seats)?
2. What will they pay per year?
3. Multiply. Then take a **believable share** (1% of TAM is not a strategy, but 1% is a sanity cap).

## Example

US dentists ≈ 200,000. Tool at $50/mo = $600/year.  
TAM ≈ 200,000 × 600 = **$120M / year**.

If you will realistically get 1,000 of them: 1,000 × 600 = **$600k ARR** ceiling for that beachhead — not $120M.

## Smell tests

- If TAM < $50M and you need a venture-scale outcome, the category may be too small (or the price is too low).
- If someone quotes $10B TAM for a niche vertical, unpack the stack. One of the factors is usually off by 10×.

## Conversation use

“There are 10k agencies, $200/mo” → 10k × 2,400 = **$24M TAM**. A $2M ARR goal is about 8% of TAM — possible, not a rounding error.

## Same idea, new numbers

- 5,000 shops × $30/mo × 12 = $1.8M TAM
- 1M users × $10/year = $10M TAM
