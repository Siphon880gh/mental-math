# Vertical and crosswise

Two-digit multiplication can stay in three slots: **ones**, **cross**, **tens**. Carry whenever a slot is 10 or more.

Modern “Vedic Mathematics” books call this Urdhva-Tiryagbhyam (“vertically and crosswise”). The arithmetic is ordinary place value written on a diagonal. You do not need the sutra story to use the slots.

That system comes from Bharati Krishna Tirtha’s 1965 book. The methods are sound; the claim that the 16 sutras come straight from ancient Vedic texts is historically disputed.

## 23 × 21

Ones: 3×1 = **3**

Cross: 2×1 + 3×2 = **8**

Tens: 2×2 = **4**

Answer: **483**

## A carry

12 × 18

Ones: 2×8 = 16 → write **6**, carry 1

Cross: 1×8 + 2×1 = 10, plus carry 1 = 11 → write **1**, carry 1

Tens: 1×1 = 1, plus carry 1 = **2**

Answer: **216**

## Three digits: five slots

The diagonal keeps going. For abc × def, each slot adds every digit pair whose places sum to that slot.

123 × 321

- Ones: 3×1 = **3**
- Tens: 2×1 + 3×2 = **8**
- Hundreds: 1×1 + 2×2 + 3×3 = 14 → write **4**, carry 1
- Thousands: 1×2 + 2×3 = 8, plus carry 1 = **9**
- Ten-thousands: 1×3 = **3**

Answer: **39,483**

The middle slot is the widest (three products). Past three digits most people switch to round-and-compensate or near-base.

## Conversation use

A 23-seat row times 21 sessions is the three-slot product, not a spreadsheet. If the numbers grow past two digits, the same diagonal pattern continues — or you switch to round-and-compensate.
