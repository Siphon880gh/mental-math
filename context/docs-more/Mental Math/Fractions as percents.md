# Fractions as percents

Memorize a **small fraction table**. Most “what percent” questions are one of these shapes, then scale.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percent | Memory |
|----------|---------|---------|--------|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% | half |
| 1/3 | 0.333… | 33⅓% | 1/3 of 300 is 100 |
| 2/3 | 0.666… | 66⅔% | twice a third |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% | quarter |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% | three quarters |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% | fifth |
| 1/6 | 0.1666… | 16⅔% | half of a third |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% | half of 25% |
| 3/8 | 0.375 | 37.5% | three eighths |
| 5/8 | 0.625 | 62.5% | five eighths |
| 7/8 | 0.875 | 87.5% | seven eighths |
| 1/9 | 0.111… | 11.1% | the digit repeats: 2/9 = 0.222… |
| 1/10 | 0.1 | 10% | tenth |
| 1/20 | 0.05 | 5% | half of 10% |
| 1/25 | 0.04 | 4% | 25 in 100 |

## Use

1/8 of 800: 12.5% of 800. 10% is 80, 2.5% is a quarter of that (20), total **100**. Or 800 / 8 = 100.

2/3 of 90: 1/3 is 30, times 2 = **60**.

1/6 of 120: a third is 40, half of that = **20**. Ninths repeat their digit: 4/9 = 0.444…, so 4/9 ≈ **44%**.

## Churn and “one over”

If monthly churn is 5% = 1/20, a rough lifetime is 20 months (see Startup Math — churn). The fraction table is how that shortcut feels obvious.

## Conversation use

“We’ll take a 25% cut” is a quarter. “They want 2/3 of the upside” is twice a third, not “about half.”

## Same idea, new numbers

- 1/5 of 350 = 70
- 3/4 of 200 = 150
- 1/8 of 2,400 = 300
- 3/8 of 80 = 30
- 3/8 as a decimal = 0.375
- 1/6 of 300 = 50
- 5/9 ≈ 0.556 ≈ 56%
