# Complements to ten and a hundred

Know instantly what is **missing**. 8 needs 2 to make 10. 37 needs 63 to make 100. 428 needs 572 to make 1,000.

That is the make-10 addition move, the pairing move in a long sum, and Gauss’s trick for 1+2+…+n.

## Complements to five

Before ten, there is five: 4 = 5 − 1, 3 = 5 − 2. Abacus learners use the five-complement so that “+4” becomes “+5, −1” on the beads. In your head it is the same split: 7 + 4 → 7 + 5 − 1 = **11**.

| Number | To 5 | To 10 |
|--------|------|-------|
| 1 | 4 | 9 |
| 2 | 3 | 8 |
| 3 | 2 | 7 |
| 4 | 1 | 6 |
| 5 | 0 | 5 |

See the Soroban and anzan guide for the bead picture.

## Make 10

8+7 → take 2 from the 7 to finish the 8, leftover 5 → **15**.

## Make 100

68+47: 68 needs 32. Take 32 from 47, leftover 15. **100+15=115**.

## Pair a list

18+72+35+65+28+82

18+82, 72+28, 35+65 → three hundreds = **300**.

## Gauss pairing

1+2+…+10: pair 1 with 10, 2 with 9, … five pairs of 11 → **55**.

In general: n(n+1)/2. 1 through 100 is 50×101 = **5,050**.

## Any even run: count × average

Gauss is a special case of **sum = number of terms × average**. In an evenly spaced run, the average is the first plus the last, halved.

20 + 21 + … + 29: ten terms, average (20+29)/2 = 24.5 → **245**.

3 + 6 + 9 + … + 30: ten terms, average 16.5 → **165**.

## Conversation use

A stack of invoices that happen to pair to $100 is a pairing sum, not a running total you might drop. “First ten days of the month numbered 1–10” is Gauss.

## Same idea, new numbers

- 9+6 = 15 (9 needs 1)
- 37 needs 63 to make 100
- 1 through 8 → 4 pairs of 9 = 36
- 428 needs 572 to make 1,000
- 6 + 3 → 6 + 5 − 2 = 9 (five-complement)
- 10 + 11 + … + 19 → 10 × 14.5 = 145
