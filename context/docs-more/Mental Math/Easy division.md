# Turn division into easier multiplication

The fastest mental divide is often **not dividing**. Rewrite the divisor as a multiply-then-shift, factor it, or scale both sides until the picture is whole.

## Rewrite table

| ÷ | Do this instead |
|---|-----------------|
| 2, 4, 8 | Halve once, twice, three times |
| 5 | ×2, then one place left |
| 20 | Halve, then one place left (or ×5, then two places left) |
| 25 | ×4, then two places left |
| 50 | ×2, then two places left |
| 125 | ×8, then three places left |
| 0.5 | ×2 |
| 0.25 | ×4 |
| 0.125 | ×8 |

675 ÷ 25 → 675 × 4 = 2,700 → **27**

## Factor the divisor

936 ÷ 12, and 12 = 3×4:

936 ÷ 3 = 312  
312 ÷ 4 = **78**

Or reverse the order: 936 ÷ 4 = 234, then 234 ÷ 3 = **78**. Pick whichever first step lands on a friendlier number.

## Missing factor

Division is multiplication run backward. Instead of 168 ÷ 12 = ?, ask **12 × what = 168?** 12 × 14 = 168, so **14**. If you know the table fact, you never divide.

## Scale both

4.8 ÷ 0.06: multiply both by 100 → 480 ÷ 6 = **80**. The quotient does not change.

Clear the divisor’s decimal first; the dividend can keep one:

- 12.6 ÷ 0.3 → ×10 → 126 ÷ 3 = **42**
- 1.5 ÷ 0.25 → ×100 → 150 ÷ 25 = **6**
- 7.2 ÷ 0.08 → ×100 → 720 ÷ 8 = **90**

## Partial quotient

936 ÷ 24: 24 × 40 = 960, one 24 too many, so 40 − 1 = **39**.

Overshoot to a round multiple, then back off. Or build up: 24 × 30 = 720, 216 left, 24 × 9 = 216 → 30 + 9 = **39**.

## Conversation use

“What’s 4.8 over six cents?” is a scale-both question, not a decimal-division ritual.

## Same idea, new numbers

- 785 ÷ 5 = 157
- 48 ÷ 0.06 → 4,800 ÷ 6 = 800 (scale ×100)
- 168 ÷ 12 = 14
- 340 ÷ 20 → 170 → 17
- 60 ÷ 0.125 → 60 × 8 = 480
- 861 ÷ 21 → 21 × 40 = 840, 21 left → 41
