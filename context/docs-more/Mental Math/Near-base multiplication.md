# Near-base multiplication

When both factors sit close to 10, 100, or 1,000, work with their **distance from the base** instead of the numbers themselves. Modern “Vedic Mathematics” books call this **Nikhilam**. The arithmetic is ordinary algebra: (B − a)(B − b) = B(B − a − b) + ab.

## Below the base

97 × 94, base 100.

- Distances: 97 is −3, 94 is −6
- Cross subtract: 97 − 6 = **91** (or 94 − 3, same thing)
- Multiply the distances: 3 × 6 = **18**
- Base 100 means the right side gets **two digits**: 91 | 18 → **9,118**

## Above the base

103 × 107, base 100.

- Distances: +3 and +7
- Cross add: 103 + 7 = **110**
- Multiply the distances: 3 × 7 = **21**
- 110 | 21 → **11,021**

## One above, one below

98 × 103, base 100.

- Distances: −2 and +3
- Cross: 98 + 3 = **101**
- Distances multiply to −2 × 3 = **−6**, so subtract instead of glue
- 101 hundreds − 6 = 10,100 − 6 = **10,094**

## Base 1,000

The right side now gets **three digits**.

996 × 988: distances −4 and −12. Cross: 996 − 12 = **984**. Product 4 × 12 = **048**. → **984,048**.

1,004 × 1,012: cross 1,016, product 048 → **1,016,048**.

## When the right side overflows

97 × 88 (base 100): cross 97 − 12 = 85, product 3 × 12 = 36 → 8,536. If the product had three digits (89 × 88: 11 × 12 = 132), carry the extra hundred left: 77 | 132 → 7,700 + 132 = **7,832**.

## A note on the name

“Vedic Mathematics” comes from Bharati Krishna Tirtha’s 1965 book. The methods are legitimate arithmetic. The claim that its 16 sutras come straight from ancient Vedic texts is historically disputed. Use the move; the label is optional.

## Conversation use

97 licenses at $94 each is 9,118. A 1,004-unit order at 1,012 grams is 1,016,048 grams — read it off the distances, no columns.

## Same idea, new numbers

- 98 × 97 → 95 | 06 = 9,506
- 102 × 104 → 106 | 08 = 10,608
- 99 × 105 → 104 hundreds − 5 = 10,395
- 995 × 998 → 993 | 010 = 993,010
