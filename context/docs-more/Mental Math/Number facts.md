# Number facts worth owning

The highest-return memory work is not only the 1–12 table. A few more lists make every other shortcut faster, because you recognize the shape instead of computing it.

## Squares 1–30

| n | n² | n | n² | n | n² |
|---|----|---|----|---|----|
| 1 | 1 | 11 | 121 | 21 | 441 |
| 2 | 4 | 12 | 144 | 22 | 484 |
| 3 | 9 | 13 | 169 | 23 | 529 |
| 4 | 16 | 14 | 196 | 24 | 576 |
| 5 | 25 | 15 | 225 | 25 | 625 |
| 6 | 36 | 16 | 256 | 26 | 676 |
| 7 | 49 | 17 | 289 | 27 | 729 |
| 8 | 64 | 18 | 324 | 28 | 784 |
| 9 | 81 | 19 | 361 | 29 | 841 |
| 10 | 100 | 20 | 400 | 30 | 900 |

Anchor on 15² = 225, 20² = 400, 25² = 625, 30² = 900. The neighbors are one step away: 26² = 625 + 25 + 26 = 676.

## Powers of 2

2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128, 256, 512, **1,024**. 2¹⁰ ≈ 1,000 is why a kilobyte is “about a thousand.”

## Powers of 10

10, 100, 1,000, 0.1, 0.01 are place shifts, not multiplies. See the Magnitude guide.

## Cubes 1–10

| n | n³ |
|---|----|
| 1 | 1 |
| 2 | 8 |
| 3 | 27 |

| 5 | 125 |
| 6 | 216 |
| 7 | 343 |

Note the overlaps: 64 is 4³ and 8², 512 is 8³ and 2⁹, 729 is 9³ and 27².

## Common reciprocals

| Fraction | Decimal |
|----------|---------|
| 1/2 | 0.5 |
| 1/3 | 0.333… |
| 1/4 | 0.25 |
| 1/5 | 0.2 |
| 1/6 | ≈ 0.1667 |
| 1/8 | 0.125 |
| 1/9 | 0.111… |
| 1/10 | 0.1 |

Dividing by 8 is multiplying by 0.125. Dividing by 6 is multiplying by about 0.1667.

## Conversation use

A 24-foot square room is 576 square feet without a pause. A 7-foot cube is 343 cubic feet. “Split six ways” is ×0.1667.
