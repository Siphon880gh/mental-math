# Area and partial products

Every multiplication is a rectangle. Split each side by place value, find each small area, and add. Long multiplication, criss-cross, line multiplication, and lattice are all this one picture drawn differently.

## Place-value expansion

Split one factor by place and multiply each part.

48 × 32 = 48 × 30 + 48 × 2 = 1,440 + 96 = **1,536**

## Partial products

Pick whichever split gives the easiest pieces.

67 × 34 = 67 × 30 + 67 × 4 = 2,010 + 268 = **2,278**

## The area grid

Split **both** factors. 23 × 14 is a 2 × 2 grid:

| × | 20 | 3 |
|---|----|---|
| 10 | 200 | 30 |
| 4 | 80 | 12 |

200 + 30 + 80 + 12 = **322**. In your head, add the big cells first: 200 + 80 = 280, then 30 + 12 = 42.

## Line multiplication

Draw each digit of one factor as a bundle of parallel lines, the other factor as crossing bundles, and count the crossing points by diagonal.

12 × 32: one line then two lines for 12; three lines then two lines crossing them for 32.

- Left group (tens × tens): 1 × 3 = **3** crossings
- Middle groups (cross terms): 1 × 2 + 2 × 3 = **8** crossings
- Right group (ones × ones): 2 × 2 = **4** crossings
- **384**

You are counting (10 + 2)(30 + 2) — the distributive law with pencils. It is clever for **seeing** why multiplication works, but slower than mental arithmetic once digits grow or carries pile up.

Its viral name, “Japanese multiplication,” has no well-established Japanese origin. Japan’s documented fast-calculation tradition is the soroban and anzan (see that guide).

## Lattice (gelosia) multiplication

Draw a grid with one cell per digit pair. Split each cell with a diagonal: tens above, ones below. Then add along the diagonals, carrying left.

23 × 14 in a lattice: cells 2×1 = 02, 3×1 = 03, 2×4 = 08, 3×4 = 12. Diagonals from the right: **2** · then 1 + 8 + 3 = 12 → write **2**, carry 1 · then 0 + 0 + 2 + 1 = **3** · then 0 → **322**.

Lattice multiplication is documented across Mediterranean and later European traditions. It is long multiplication with the carries postponed — useful on paper, not a head move.

## Conversation use

“48 people at $32” is 1,440 + 96 = **$1,536**. Say the big partial product first; the small one is a correction.

- 54 × 13 → 540 + 162 = 702
- 25 × 32 grid → 600 + 40 + 150 + 10 = 800
- 21 × 13 line → 2 | 7 | 3 = 273
