# Multiply near ten or a hundred

When a factor sits next to 10 or 100, use **(round number) × other, then adjust** — or a tiny algebraic shape you can feel without naming algebra.

## ×9 is ×10 minus the number

47 × 9 = 470 − 47 = **423**

## ×11 (two-digit)

Add the two digits and put the sum in the middle. If the sum is 10 or more, carry 1 to the left digit.

35 × 11 → 3, (3+5)=8, 5 → **385**  
48 × 11 → 4, 12, 8 → carry → **528**

## Near 100

98 × 97

Think: 98 is 100 − 2, 97 is 100 − 3.

- Cross subtract: 98 − 3 = 95 (or 97 − 2 = 95) — that is the left part (hundreds/thousands depending on scale)
- Multiply the “holes”: 2 × 3 = 6
- **9,506** (95 hundreds + 06)

You only need this shape for numbers in the 90s. For 102 × 104, add the extra: 102 + 4 = 106, holes 2 × 4 = 8 → **10,608**.

## Squares ending in 5

n5²: take n × (n+1), then glue 25.

35² → 3 × 4 = 12, glue 25 → **1,225**  
75² → 7 × 8 = 56, glue 25 → **5,625**

## Conversation use

A 9% discount is “ten percent minus one percent.” A 2-year-old “eleven times” joke in a pricing grid is the middle-digit trick. $35 seat × 11 months (promo year) is 385.
