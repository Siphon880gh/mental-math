# Soroban and anzan

Japan’s documented rapid-calculation tradition is the **soroban** (abacus) and **anzan**, calculating on a pictured abacus in your head. Experts are not saying “47 plus 68” to themselves. They move imagined beads.

You do not need a soroban to borrow the core move: **complements to 5 and to 10**.

## The rod

Each soroban rod is one place value. One upper bead is worth 5. Four lower beads are worth 1 each. So every digit 0–9 is “some fives plus some ones.” A bead counts when it is pushed against the beam (highlighted below).

::soroban 7 | 7 on one rod: the five bead plus two ones.
::soroban 3 | 3: three ones, one lower bead still free.
::soroban 9 | 9: the five plus all four ones. The rod is full.

## Complement to five

When the lower beads run out, add a five and take some ones back.

- +4 is **+5, −1**
- +3 is **+5, −2**
- −4 is **−5, +1**

SAMPLE: 3 + 4. The lower rod has 3 ones and only 1 free bead. Push the five down, take one away → **7**.

::soroban 3 | Before: 3. Only one lower bead is free, so +4 cannot go on directly.
::soroban 7 | After +5, −1: the five is in, one lower bead came off.

## Complement to ten

When the rod is full, carry one to the next rod and take the complement back.

- +9 is **+10, −1**
- +8 is **+10, −2**
- +7 is **+10, −3**
- +6 is **+10, −4**

SAMPLE: 47 + 8 → 47 + 10 − 2 = **55**. The tens rod gains one; the ones rod loses two.

::soroban 47 | Before: 47. The ones rod shows 7 and cannot take 8 more.
::soroban 55 | After +10, −2: one bead on the tens rod, two off the ones rod.

## Taking away: the mirror moves

Subtraction runs the same complements backwards.

- Enough beads on the rod: take them straight off. 8 − 3 → **5**.
- Not enough lower beads: take the five off and give the complement back. −4 is **−5, +1**, so 7 − 4 → **3**.
- The rod would go below 0: borrow one from the next rod and give the ten-complement back. −6 is **−10, +4**.

SAMPLE: 52 − 6 → 52 − 10 + 4 = **46**. The tens rod loses one; the ones rod gains four. Adding those four to a rod showing 2 is itself a five-complement: +5, −1.

::soroban 52 | Before: 52. The ones rod shows 2 and cannot give up 6.
::soroban 46 | After −10, +4: one bead off the tens rod, the ones rod now shows 6.

## Anzan: the same moves, no beads

After enough practice the bead picture moves without hands. The trainer cannot give you that picture, but it can drill the reflex underneath: hear “+8” and think “+10, −2” without counting. The Soroban rod game shows the beads move for each add, so you can check the picture you are forming.

## Older cousins: counting rods and the suanpan

China calculated on **counting rods** laid out on a place-value board, with algorithms for multiplication, division, and square roots. Moving a rod one column was ×10 — the same idea as the Magnitude guide. The later Chinese **suanpan** (a bead abacus, usually 2 upper and 5 lower beads) became a fast calculating tool and influenced the soroban.

These are history and apparatus. The mental moves they leave behind are place-value shifts and complements.

## Conversation use

“We had 38 signups and got 7 more” is 38 + 10 − 3 = **45**. “Take 6 off 52” is 52 − 10 + 4 = **46**. The complement is faster than counting up.

## Same idea, new numbers

- +4 on a rod showing 2 → +5, −1 → 6
- 56 + 9 → 56 + 10 − 1 = 65
- 73 − 8 → 73 − 10 + 2 = 65
- 4 needs 1 to make 5; 4 needs 6 to make 10
