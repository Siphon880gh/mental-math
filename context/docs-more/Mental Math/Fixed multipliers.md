# Fixed multipliers

Some multipliers show up so often that each one deserves a reflex. Every row below turns the multiplier into a double, a shift, or a small add or subtract.

## Three families inside the table

**Neighbors of a round number.** ×9, ×19, ×99, ×999 are “round, then minus one copy.” ×11, ×101, ×1,001 are “round, then plus one copy.” 42 × 19 = 840 − 42 = **798**.

**Fractions of a power of ten.** ×5 = ×10 ÷ 2. ×25 = ×100 ÷ 4. ×50 = ×100 ÷ 2. ×75 = ×100 × ¾. ×125 = ×1,000 ÷ 8. You trade a multiply for a shift and an easy divide.

**Small builds.** ×3 = ×2 + ×1. ×6 = ×5 + ×1. ×12 = ×10 + ×2. ×15 = ×10 + ×5 (and ×5 is half of ×10). ×18 = ×20 − ×2.

## Eleven, all the way

Two digits: put the digit sum in the middle. 43 × 11 → 4 | 4+3 | 3 → **473**. Carry when the middle is 10+.

Longer numbers: first digit, each adjacent pair sum, last digit. 347 × 11 → 3 | 7 | 11 | 7 → carry → **3,817**. This neighbor rule is the simplest of the Trachtenberg-style digit rules.

## ×1001 and ×101 park copies

38 × 1,001 = 38,000 + 38 = **38,038** — the number written twice with a zero gap. 47 × 101 = **4,747**, the two-digit number written twice.

## Conversation use

$37 a seat for a 12-person team is 370 + 74 = **$444**. A $19 add-on for 42 accounts is 840 − 42 = **$798**. A 15% fee on $46 is the ×15 row shifted: 6.90.
