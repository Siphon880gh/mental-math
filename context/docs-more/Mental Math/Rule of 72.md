# Rule of 72

For **compound** growth (not linear “+100 users/month”), doubling time in years is about:

> 72 ÷ annual percent rate

## Examples

- 6%/year → 72/6 = **12 years** to double
- 9%/year → 72/9 = **8 years**
- 12%/year → 72/12 = **6 years**
- 36%/year (aggressive fund) → 72/36 = **2 years**

This is an approximation. It is good enough to reject “we’ll 10× in two years at 15%.”

## What it is not

- Not for **monthly** 20% startup growth — that is the 20% table in the original ladder (≈3× in 6 months, ≈9× in 12).
- Not for simple interest (no compounding).

## Reverse

To double in 5 years you need about 72/5 ≈ **14% per year** compounded.

## Conversation use

Someone says cash in a 4% account doubles in five years. 72/4 = 18 years, not five. Smell test failed.

## Same idea, new numbers

- 8% → 9 years
- Double in 10 years → about 7.2%/year
- 24% → 3 years
