# Pair the easy factors first

Multiplication is associative and commutative. The order you *write* is not the order you should compute.

Scan for a pair that makes 10, 100, or 1,000, then dump whatever is left onto that round number.

## Associate to 100

25 × 16 × 4

Do not start 25×16. Pair 25×4 = **100**, then 100×16 = **1,600**.

## Scan the whole product

8 × 37 × 125

8×125 = 1,000. Then ×37 = **37,000**.

Left-to-right 8×37 first is the slow path on purpose.

## Distribute when one factor is a split

Break one factor into easy pieces and multiply each piece:

23 × 17 = 23×10 + 23×7 = 230 + 161 = **391**

Use this after you decided *not* to regroup. Factoring and distributing are the same habit: rewrite before you grind. The Area and partial products guide draws this as a rectangle.

## Factor the multiplier

Replace an awkward multiplier with its factors and apply them one at a time.

×18 = ×9 then ×2. 37 × 18 → 37 × 9 = 333 → ×2 = **666**.

×12 = ×3 then ×4. ×16 = four doubles. ×45 = ×9 then ×5.

## Flip the product

a × b = b × a. 17 × 6 can be “six 17s” (102) or “seventeen 6s” (60 + 42 = 102). Say whichever version has the easier pieces.

The same commutativity powers the percent swap: 4% of 75 is 75% of 4 = **3**.

## Conversation use

Headcount 8 × $37 × 125 seats is “eight times an eighth of a thousand, times 37,” not three ugly multiplies.

## Same idea, new numbers

- 25 × 48 = 25×4×12 = 100×12 = 1,200
- 5 × 17 × 2 = 10×17 = 170
- 125 × 16 × 8 = 1,000×16 = 16,000
- 24 × 18 → 24 × 9 = 216, ×2 = 432
- 6 × 23 → 23 × 6 = 120 + 18 = 138
